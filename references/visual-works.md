# Visual works and authoring products

Use for code-rendered images, share cards, procedural/interactive scenes,
temporal works, or products that create artifacts. For native document, slide,
SVG, image, or video production, compose with the available specialized method
when relevant; this reference does not replace that artifact's renderer.

## Separate the work from the workspace

Establish the work's reader, publication surface, reading size, and intended
takeaway or experience. Establish the workspace's task, current object,
editing scope, and feedback separately. A calm editor can author an intense
work; a vivid work does not authorize decorative complexity in every control.

Use representative content from the product's core work types. One attractive
cover must not quietly redefine a diagramming or publishing tool as a poster
maker. Replace the sample with meaningfully different authored content before
claiming that the format generalizes. Choose revealing short, long, or absent
content where it could change composition; do not multiply every field into a
ritual test matrix. A dashboard and a share card need their own composition
even if they share a brand. Validate both the authoring workflow and its actual
output.

## Choose the kind of visual change deliberately

Palettes vary color. Visual languages may change type roles, node/component
forms, strokes, annotation conventions, and spatial expression. Preserve
semantic relationships and authored constraints. Existing theme, layout,
content, and publication dimensions keep their own authority; a coordinated
preset may compose those owners, but must not hide structural or semantic
mutations inside tokens.

To compare visual languages, hold content and palette steady where possible.
To compare palettes, keep structure and typography stable. A claimed language
needs graphic differences beyond recoloring. Keep interface language and work
language independent where bilingual authoring is supported; validate real
labels and mixed-script content in each affected surface.

Workspace styling must not leak into the work's renderer. Check shared or
inherited font, text-rendering, smoothing, color, and scale rules when a UI polish
changes preview or export. Reuse the canonical render path; avoid a special
showcase renderer that makes examples better than the product can reproduce.

## Solve the expressive center

Determine which quality makes this work worth viewing: spatial depth, optical
material, editorial rhythm, a revealing relationship, motion, or response to
touch. Render that quality early. Do not surround an unresolved subject with
settings, labels, frames, and controls and call the experience complete.

For a material or atmosphere, inspect the relationships that produce it:
lighting direction, value range, edge behavior, overlap, scale cues, movement,
and background contrast. For a graphic composition, inspect relative masses,
crop, text rhythm, grouping, and the intended reading sequence. These are
diagnostic handles, not requirements to add each effect.

Development controls, repeatable scenes, and parameter comparisons can shorten
authoring. Choose reliable defaults before delivery. Remove temporary tuning
UI from the user path unless those controls have a real creative purpose; do
not make the user finish the art direction by turning fifteen knobs. Preserve
requested creative controls and their real behavior.

## Carry the authored subject into the composition

Distinguish reusable composition from subject-specific material. A shared grid,
type system, or abstract motif can serve many subjects well. A literal scene,
illustration, or sample slogan may instead carry meaning that contradicts a
replacement subject. Text fitting inside the same boxes proves less than the
work still communicating the intended message.

- **Fixed illustration.** A baked scene carries a specific subject (a season, an
  occasion, an open book). Decide whether it is a neutral ground or the work's
  subject. If a replacement topic makes its meaning inappropriate, use the
  product's supported selection, omission, or replacement behavior, or develop
  the missing capability when it belongs to the requested tool. Do not pretend
  a fixed illustration adapts automatically. An unillustrated alternative still
  needs a considered composition; removing the scene must not leave a hole.
- **Recurring marks.** A brand rule, footer line, or repeated motif may stay
  fixed; decide deliberately, and do not let it become the only visual anchor
  on a wide empty work.

Judge each issue as functional, craft, content fitness, or personal taste;
these carry different authority. An authored control that is missing, dead, or
mislabeled, copy that does not survive an edit, and text clipped out of the
frame are functional. Hierarchy, type scale, spacing, and the relation between
graphic and copy are craft judgments. Whether it still suits an unrelated,
mundane, or unexpected subject is content fitness. Which of two working illustrations you
prefer can depend on taste. The agent remains responsible for grounded craft
and content-fit judgments before handoff; owner approval is separate. Keep
judgments and their uncertainty explicit, and add no universal rule that an
accent, gradient, or illustration is forbidden.

## Turn visual exploration into product capability

When the task is to develop a creative tool, use a small set of meaningfully
different inputs to expose missing capabilities. Distinguish visual situations,
not just filenames: a distant small subject, a close portrait, busy edges, soft
light, or substantial negative space may need different treatment. Choose only
contrasts relevant to this product; do not build a ritual sample matrix.

First make the visual relationship work in the real renderer. Then identify the
controllable cause: foreground/background relation, crop, text mass, curvature,
edge treatment, density, or timing. Return that mechanism to the existing model,
renderer, and usable controls when it belongs to the requested product. Preserve
authored choices during unrelated edits and procedural variation.

After a study, distinguish what became reproducible through the product, what
remains a manually authored research result, and what did not justify a new
capability. An attractive hand-tuned example does not prove an automatic
generator achieves the same quality. Verify a claimed capability from its
ordinary entrypoint with another relevant input; do not hide private repair
steps or artifact-only scripts behind a product-complete claim.

Expose promised examples as findable, editable works when the product supports
that contract. Keep original assets, projects, and exports distinguishable.
Repeated requests for new studies should advance the creative tool, rather
than gradually turning development into an unrelated image-finishing service.
Use [app interaction](app-interaction.md) for object, action, and state ownership.

## Judge the actual medium

| Medium | Observe |
| --- | --- |
| Static share image / graphic | Actual export dimensions, legibility at viewing size, crop, font/asset output, clear takeaway |
| Interactive procedural scene | Initial render, expressive subject, user response, resting state, and meaningful performance constraints |
| Animation / loop | Entry, progression, interaction response, representative phases, and loop seam; a single attractive frame is insufficient |
| Editor output | Workspace clarity plus exported/published artifact; preview alone cannot prove output fidelity |
| Sound with visuals, when in scope | Actual timing and controllable playback in the target runtime; do not claim audio QA from a screenshot |

Match comparison conditions and input/seed when available. Static capture and
temporal observation answer different questions. Nonessential motion should
respect user preferences; reducing it should keep understandable state and a
considered resting composition. Essential expressive motion needs an honest,
appropriate control/alternative within the product contract, not an unsupported
claim of blanket accessibility conformance.

Inspect the requested positive quality as well as defects. “Buttons work” is
insufficient evidence for a work whose central promise is material or rhythm.
Conversely, aesthetic acceptance does not prove a user can operate an editor.
State the observed quality and its limits; do not manufacture an aesthetic score.
