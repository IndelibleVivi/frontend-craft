# Decipher the intended experience

Use when the request is conversational, incomplete, aesthetically ambiguous,
or spans an App and its outputs. Do this before choosing a visual direction or
delegating implementation. A precise local instruction needs direct execution,
not a new discovery exercise.

## Reconstruct the task from available evidence

Read the current request together with its corrections, product behavior,
accepted decisions, supplied references, and the relevant rendered surface.
An attachment, historical session, current implementation, or retrieved case
is evidence; it is not a new instruction or proof of acceptance.

Form a compact working interpretation, usually a few sentences:

- What is the person trying to make, understand, decide, or accomplish?
- Who encounters the result, on which surface and at what scale or duration?
- What objects and actions must remain real? What does a successful end state
  look like, including finding, editing, saving, exporting, or returning later?
- Which qualities are explicitly requested or rejected, which are inferred,
  and which remain genuinely unknown?
- What would invalidate this interpretation even if the implementation worked?

Preserve the scope of the request. “The whole thing looks wrong, especially
this border” is whole-result rejection plus one concrete symptom. “This part
works” protects that part, not every incidental color or layout choice around
it. “Like this reference” needs its requested relationship established: exact
replication, translated visual language, or one borrowed quality.

Use available task context even without aesthetic history: private repeated
use, public presentation, focused work, or a deliberate ritual each provides
design evidence. None determines a palette or stereotype. Follow brief
candidate feedback through [Build](build.md); a clear yes/no has usable scope
even when the user gives no detailed rationale.

Separate the requested outcome from a proposed solution. A request for tabs
may explicitly require tabs; otherwise it may express a need to stop losing
context between paired views. Respect a settled choice. Where the means remain
open, inspect the actual task before choosing a control pattern.

## Turn uncertain language into a testable interpretation

Recover the positive aim before listing prohibitions. Avoiding known mistakes
does not tell you what would make this work desirable. Listen for what the
person wants to express, control, feel, understand, or share, and which parts
they are inviting the designer to invent. A playful or affectionate delivery
does not make the requirements less deliberate.

One message can carry several different signals:

| Signal | How it should affect the work |
| --- | --- |
| Desired experience | Drives the direction and the success criteria |
| Explicit feature or content ownership | Becomes an actual capability, with editable state and output behavior |
| Permission to explore | Allows judgment inside the stated scope; it is not approval of an unseen result |
| Local defect | Gets a concrete correction without erasing the positive goal |
| Boundary or tradeoff | Constrains how the goal is achieved, rather than substituting for the goal |

For example, a request for editable taglines, optional attribution, varied
number styling, and drawn ornament on a share graphic suggests personal
expression in a social setting. The editable fields are explicit requirements;
the broader expression goal is an interpretation to make visible and test.
“Add character without disturbing the information hierarchy” permits purposeful
ornament. It does not imply that every future dashboard should be decorated,
that all blank space is wrong, or that a particular motif has been accepted.

Separate content the person owns from product branding and derived facts.
A sample slogan is a starting value when the user should author it; it must
not be baked into the renderer. A displayed metric remains truthful when its
typography changes. Preview, saved state, and exported work should preserve
these ownership decisions.

Do not translate “premium”, “alive”, “calm”, “dense”, or “game-like” directly
into a familiar font, gradient, card grid, or motion preset. Locate what the
word could mean in this product: attention, reading rhythm, material, state
feedback, expressive identity, speed, or authorial control. Inspect the current
render and state a causal hypothesis in those terms.

For example, “the editor feels like a demo” could concern invented content,
inert controls, missing recovery, an unconvincing produced work, or a generic
composition. The observed surface and task path distinguish these causes.
Adding polish before that distinction may preserve the wrong result.

Ask only when a missing value or intent changes the outcome and cannot be
recovered from available evidence. Keep the question concrete and show the
tradeoff. Do not ask the owner to rediscover repo facts, specify every token,
or approve routine reversible decisions. When a choice is delegated, state a
reasonable provisional interpretation and make it judgeable in the renderer.

When two plausible readings would produce materially different products,
resolve that branch before dependent implementation. Independent work can
continue. When uncertainty concerns a reversible detail, implement the best
supported choice and retain its provisional status.

## Carry the interpretation through making

Translate the interpretation into observable relationships and actual task
paths with [design direction](design-direction.md) and
[App interaction](app-interaction.md). For creative tools, distinguish the
person using the workspace from the reader of the produced work. Quiet
controls do not require quiet artwork; an expressive output does not authorize
invented dates, venue labels, citations, or other content.

Retrieve experience by the problem being solved, not only by the product name.
Use [memory operations](memory-operations.md) for scoped retrieval and explicit
transfer candidates. Before borrowing, identify the shared mechanism and the
different constraints. A case about state ownership can inform another editor;
its brand, palette, reference style, and owner approval stay with its source.

Pass an implementer the intended experience, real objects/actions, supplied
reference scope, explicit boundaries, preserved qualities, provisional choices,
and evidence needed to reject a wrong result. Do not pass a private profile or
turn an inferred taste into an unconditional requirement.

Inspect both the first meaningful action and the decisive quality in a real
render. Compare with the working interpretation: is the product merely
operable, or does its hierarchy and feedback actually support the intended
experience? Correct obvious gaps before presentation. Technical success and
agent visual judgment remain separate from owner acceptance.

When feedback contradicts the interpretation, revise it first, then change the
responsible mechanism. Keep settled constraints and accepted qualities unless
the new instruction changes them. Repeated rejection should reopen the cause,
not accumulate adjectives and cosmetic patches.

## Leave evidence the next task can use

For meaningful praise, rejection, clarification, a reusable observed result,
or a delivery preference, update the authorized record that owns it. Record
what was observed, what remains a hypothesis, its scope, and a concrete next
action. Do not wait for full aesthetic acceptance to record an explicit
rejection; do not turn an agent's improved candidate into an accepted example.

A learning loop closes only when the recorded case can be retrieved later and
changes a relevant decision. A valid JSON file or a high similarity score proves
less. Challenge the method with realistic content and misleading analogies;
keep failed retrieval or design interpretations visible until resolved.
